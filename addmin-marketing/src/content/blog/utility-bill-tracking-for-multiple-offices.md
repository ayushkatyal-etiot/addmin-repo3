If you're running two or three offices, a spreadsheet with due dates in it is probably fine. Someone checks it every
Monday, chases whoever needs to pay, and the bills get paid on time.

Past that point, it usually starts breaking down — not all at once, but one missed bill at a time.

## Why it breaks down

**Every office invents its own system.** Office A tracks its electricity bill in a shared sheet. Office B's admin
prefers a WhatsApp reminder to themselves. Office C doesn't track it at all — they just wait for the invoice email
and hope they see it in time. There's no single view of what's coming due across all of them, so nobody notices when
one office quietly falls behind.

**A spreadsheet doesn't know a bill is coming — it only remembers dates you've already typed in.** It can't tell you
"this bill hasn't shown up yet and it's usually here by now." It just sits there until someone opens it and checks,
and on a busy week, nobody does.

**Handoffs lose context.** When the person who built the spreadsheet goes on leave, is on a different shift, or
leaves the company, the tracking goes with them. The next person inherits a file of columns with no explanation of
which ones actually matter.

This is exactly the pattern behind one of the most common complaints we hear from admin teams running more than one
office: *"the electricity board called about a cut-off before Admin even knew the bill existed."* By the time
someone notices, it's already a fire to put out, not a bill to pay on schedule.

## What actually fixes it

The fix isn't a better spreadsheet template — it's treating "a bill is expected" as something the system tracks on
its own, not something a person has to remember to check.

That means:

- **One place for every office**, not a different tracker per location.
- **A recurring schedule per utility connection**, so the system already knows a bill is due before it arrives — it
  doesn't wait for someone to type in next month's date by hand.
- **A visible flag the moment something's overdue**, not a silent gap in a spreadsheet column.
- **Handoff-proof by design** — because the tracking lives in a shared system, not one person's file, a new admin
  can see exactly what's outstanding on day one.
